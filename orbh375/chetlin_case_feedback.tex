Reflect upon and review your experiences in this class thus far in the semester.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Have any activities or conversations been particularly beneficial to your development?
        If so, which ones and how have they affected you?

        I've enjoyed the few discussions I've had with Lauren about the distinction between self-awareness and active self improvement throughout the semester.
        We seem to have a similar kind of self-awareness and self-familiarity, and she's pointed out that such a state doesn't necessarily mean we're working on improving ourselves.

  \item Discuss ways your experiences have affected your thinking, changed attitudes, or made you re-examine assumptions.

        I've been actively thinking about the ideas in care ethics since we read about it.
        The definitions of caring for vs. caring about put words to a pattern I had noticed in my life prior, and I've been trying to apply these ideas to evaluate how I can better care for others and how others can better care for me.

  \item Describe ways in which your experiences translated into changes made in your life outside of class.

        To be honest, I'm not sure I've made any one massive change to my life or behavior, but I've definitely had a number of small perspective shifts.
        The care ethics idea has definitely been something to consider.
        Additionally, one of the notes I got from my ethical leader film presentation was that my vocal delivery was smooth and confident but my body language betrayed some discomfort or nervousness; I've been trying to stay conscious of this.

  \item What 2-3 readings or chapters have you liked the best or learned the most from?
        Explain what you liked and a key learning from each.

        As I mentioned, I really enjoyed the care ethics reading (Noddings).
        In particular, the key insight for me was the distinction between caring \textit{about} and taking care \textit{of}, which are differentiated by the requirement that the latter involves a feedback loop with the cared \textit{for}.
        I also appreciated the Ladkin chapter on listening styles and asking artful questions, along with the listening quiz.
        I was reminded that often my active listening habits can be detrimental to the total comprehension, and have been keeping that in the back of my mind recently.

  \item What is working well for you so far?

        I generally like the class format.
        It does well for me to have an open dialogue, especially as compared to my myriad STEM, lecture based classes.
        I also appreciate that the class has a component of reviewing the assigned readings from the perspective of the instructor, as it helps me check my understanding and reinforce the important points.

  \item If you could enhance your learning experience, what would you change?

        I would prefer weekly or biweekly written assignments referring to the readings and in-class dialogues instead of the journal.
        I have trouble keeping up with long-term assignments which have no (or few) interim deadlines, and as such had to write a fairly sizable section of my journal in the week or so leading up to the first check in.

  \item On a five-point scale, with five high (an ``A''), how would you rate the course and the instructor?

        I would rate the course a 3, and the instructor a 5.

  \item Explain your rating. What else is needed for you to rate both the course and the instructor a 5?

        Its hard for me to completely explain my course rating.
        I think its possible I just prefer more theoretical classes/classes which are more about learning external concepts and less about internal ideas.
        This isn't really an indictment of any aspect of the class itself, just a personal preference.
        As for the instructor, I really appreciate her clear engagement and passion for teaching, and how consistent and understanding her communication is.
        No notes to that point.

\end{enumerate}
